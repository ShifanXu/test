Research

Architectures, memory systems, and fault tolerance for quantum computing.

Research Areas

Doctoral research at Yale University, 2021–Present · Graduate Research Assistant, 2023–Present

Quantum Random Access Memory Architecture

I study how quantum random access memory should be designed as a practical subsystem inside a larger fault-tolerant machine. This includes questions of bandwidth, sharing, routing, organization, defect tolerance, and structured data access. More broadly, I am interested in when quantum memory architectures are actually useful at system scale, and what architectural assumptions make them practical.

During my 2025 research internship at Pacific Northwest National Laboratory with Ang Li, I worked on oblivious quantum queries, resulting in OQRAM: Oblivious Quantum Random Access Memory for Securing Delegated Quantum Queries.

Fault-Tolerant Magic-State Preparation

I work on how non-Clifford resources should be prepared under realistic fault-tolerant architectural constraints. This includes magic-state injection, distillation, scheduling, and subsystem integration, especially in settings where code structure and connectivity strongly affect cost. A recurring theme is that magic-state preparation should be viewed as an architectural problem rather than only a protocol-design problem.

Error-Correction-Aware Architecture

A broader theme in my work is that error correction cannot be treated as a black box beneath the architecture. Different codes expose different constraints on layout, routing, movement, and resource sharing, and those differences should shape architectural decisions from the start. I am interested in how code choices and system organization can be coordinated so that fault tolerance becomes a design principle rather than a downstream constraint.

Full-Stack qLDPC Resource Estimation

My ongoing work connects quantum algorithms to their implementation costs on qLDPC architectures. A current focus is DF-THC Hamiltonian simulation on neutral-atom systems, including logical-qubit mapping and magic-state delivery. We have preliminary resource estimates for FeMoco simulation and are developing the broader algorithm-to-architecture analysis.

AI for Quantum Architecture

I am exploring AI-assisted architecture design and fault-tolerant circuit optimization, including automated syndrome-extraction design. This is an emerging direction within my broader work on quantum computer architecture.

Research Software and Tutorials

QRAM in PennyLane

I collaborated with Xanadu on QRAM implementations in PennyLane and coauthored Intro to quantum random access memory (QRAM) with David Ren, Marcus Edwards, and Yongshan Ding (August 2026). The tutorial uses executable examples to compare select-only, bucket-brigade, and hybrid QRAM constructions and their qubit-count and circuit-depth tradeoffs.

Research Perspective

Across these projects, I am interested in the systems bottlenecks that separate abstract quantum algorithms from deployable large-scale machines. My work tries to connect architectural abstractions with realistic error-corrected implementations and quantitative resource tradeoffs.

The common theme is that scalable quantum computing will depend not only on better codes or better devices, but also on better subsystem interfaces, memory organizations, and full-stack evaluation methodologies. I am especially interested in questions where architectural structure changes what is realistically implementable, and in how AI-assisted exploration can support this design process.
